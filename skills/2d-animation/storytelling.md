# 2D ANIMATION + STORY — A CARTOON FILM TOLD BY ITS VOICES

## Your scene
One drawn shot of the film: the world in three layers, the people staged and acting with the kit (`ZC`), the camera moving,
the action on its wordLocks. A B-roll: the thing, the idea or the cast on a ground made for the moment (core-graphic §3b). A
card scene: a cartoon card in the same style, its words popping in on their wordLocks.
<!-- both -->
## The 2D checks (the art director's eye, on every look at the frames)
1. **The place matches the story**: a race on a race track, a jacket shop with jacket racks; it looks the same in every
   shot of that place.
2. **Hook and pace**: 0:00 opens on action or a face; shot duration follows the event; no mechanical cutting schedule.
3. **Bodies and staging**: apply the canonical anatomy contract; judge composition and blocking against the shot plan.
4. **A living world**: far, middle and front layers; people in public places; something always moves.
5. **Mouths**: only the speaker's mouth moves; on a narrator line no mouth moves.
6. **Readable**: captions and text never on a face; signs in the story's language; nothing important under the captions.
7. **The look**: one cartoon style (flat colour, soft shading, no dark outlines, warm colours) in every shot; no glass, no neon.
8. **Worth watching**: it flows, the feeling reads on the faces, the payoff lands.

## Physical performance review
Apply H01–H21 and the evidence/acceptance process in [the canonical anatomy contract](human-anatomy-rigging.md#14-pose-tests-and-review-acceptance).
Compare actual action with the authored shot plan. For shot coverage, verify OTS/reverse geography, eyelines, screen
direction and cut continuity. Intentional foreground shoulders and detail crops are valid compositions.
