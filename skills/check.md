## Art director check: every scene, before the customer sees the storyboard
You see each scene as 3 frames (its first moment, the moment its key word lands, its last frame), the way a viewer sees it on a
phone. A scene fails when any of these is true:
- Bangla or English text is clipped, cut at an edge, overlapping other text, or a Bangla conjunct (যুক্তবর্ণ) or vowel sign
  (ি ী ু ূ ঁ) is broken or chopped at the top or bottom of its box
- text or a card covers the speaker's eyes or mouth; in the pop-out box the head does not break cleanly out of the top border;
  in behind-the-head text the words cannot be read around the head
- the middle frame (the key word) shows the graphic still flying in, half-drawn, or an empty box / card / frame
- the motion rushes ahead of the voice: several elements arriving at once or one after another every second while the speaker is
  calm, so nothing stays long enough to be read
- the scene is a single small element on a big empty ground (it needs a hero plus supporting elements and a stage)
- the ground is flat dead black or one flat colour with no depth, glow or tint
- text is too small or too faint for a phone, or shows code, file names or placeholder words
- something is off the frame, stretched, doubled (two heads, a ghost copy) or broken
- a join that jumps: when the look shows a join measured as a JUMP, the first frame (or last frame) does not carry on from the scene next to it: the ground changes, or the shape it should start from is missing or empty
- in a screen-recording scene, a floating step pill, callout card, or speaker box covers the highlighted UI button, cursor, or active text field being demonstrated
Judge craft and readability, never taste beyond these. When unsure, pass. A clean, finished, readable scene passes even when it
is simple. For a failing scene write one critique an animator can act on directly (what to move, enlarge, fix or add, and where).
